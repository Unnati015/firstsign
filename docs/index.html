FirstSign

Instant sick pay for India's 40 crore informal workers, and the earliest warning of every disease outbreak.

The problem, plainly

When a disease spreads through a neighbourhood, dengue, malaria or typhoid in the monsoon, heatstroke in summer, a daily-wage worker who falls sick loses a week's income and no one pays him: every fever policy in India pays only after a hospital admission, and most fever is treated at home. Meanwhile the health system learns about the spread from hospital reports, about ten days late.

How it works

  1. ₹19 a month (₹199/yr) covers a worker, usually paid by a gig platform, a welfare board or an employer.
  2. He tests positive on a rapid test for dengue, malaria or typhoid, at a chemist or with an ASHA.
  3. He photographs it in the app; AI checks the test, the pharmacy bill or ASHA record, and the pin code.
  4. ₹3,000 lands on UPI the same day, roughly six lost days' wages.
  5. Each payout is a verified case on a live map. The pin-code Outbreak Index shows officials a cluster forming days before hospitals report it, and test kits go to every covered worker in a red pin code.

Break-even at about 3 million workers (year 3); ₹34 crore of fees at 10 million, plus data revenue.

Our weakest assumptions, which the pilot (50,000 workers, one monsoon) exists to measure: (1) the claim rate; (2) fraud on test photos (bill QR, geo and time checks, one claim per season, 15-day wait); (3) whether platforms spend their social-security money on this; (4) chemists' cooperation.
