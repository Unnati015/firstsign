FirstSign

Outbreak cover that pays on the first sign, not the last.

The idea in five lines

  1. Problem. For ~40 crore daily-wage and gig workers, a week of dengue or viral fever is a week without income. Every dengue policy in India pays only on hospital admission, and most dengue is treated at home. The state learns of outbreaks about ten days late: 297 dengue deaths counted vs 2,340 on death certificates (2024).
  2. Product. ₹199 a year (₹19/month). When your pin code's Outbreak Index turns red, a test-and-protect kit reaches you. If the test is positive, photograph the strip in the app and ₹3,000 lands on your UPI the same day. No hospital, no forms.
  3. The twist. Every payout is a geo-tagged report, so the more people are covered, the earlier an outbreak is seen. Surveillance funded by premiums, not budgets.
  4. Sentinel pharmacies. 10 lakh ASHAs cover the villages; 12 lakh chemists cover everyone else. FirstSign pays chemists per verified report, like an ASHA task incentive.
  5. Business. We build the index, verification and payout rails; a licensed insurer carries the risk; wallets, telcos and gig platforms sell it. ₹20 Cr premium at 1M workers; break-even around year 3.

Our weakest assumptions — the pilot exists to measure them: (1) the 50–60% loss ratio; (2) fraud on strip-photo claims (mitigations: pharmacy bill QR + geo + one claim per season + random tele-verification); (3) chemists' willingness to share daily category sales; (4) basis risk on the area trigger (kept small and in-kind).
